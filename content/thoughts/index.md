## November 13, 2026
Some preregistered predictions:
- LLMs don't have the juice (there will not be an intelligence explosion in 2027)
- AI will prove/disprove the Riemann hypothesis by the end of 2027

I make the first because I think people are [significantly overupdating](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q35-timelines-update-looks-like-the) in response to METR's [recent assessment](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/) of AI R&D acceleration at Anthropic. I largely think LLMs are still [driven by imitation learning](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wYpjXRLqbLbnmjbJP/llms-are-still-mostly-powered-by-imitative-learning-not-rl#comments) and that we're still in more of a [prosaic RSI](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LP6uCXs6Ea5qSbWpY/twenty-years-from-rsi-to-takeoff-slow-learning-scaling) timeline. I have little confidence beyond 2027.

I make the second because of a silly Instagram Reel that I watched bemoaning the downfall of math as we know it. It began by just discussing some of the recently AI-proven math results---this part was fine---but then prediced that AI would solve the Riemann hypothesis in 2039, which is utterly ridiculous. As probably the most well-known open math problem, I think it's likely to be solved (relatively) quite soon.


## October 5, 2026

You might be able to mildly improve your quality of life by using more sacrificial barriers. We already do this with items like screen protectors, pillowcases, and garbage bags, but we can actually take the last two further: pillowcases and garbage bags have essentially no influence on the effectiveness of pillows and garbage bins, so why not use two of them at once? That way, if the outer cover gets dirty/rips/etc., any mess is caught by the easy-to-clean/replace inner cover, not the hard-to-clean item that the covers protect.

## July 14, 2026

I've been thinking more about interpretability lately. I've long told people that I'm not particularly drawn to mechanistic interpretability, but that I do like interpretability in general, and it seems this has become increasingly the case. There is also a new brand of interpretability techniques, though, that I am also not super drawn to; I call them "uninterpretable interpretability" techniques and wrote some thoughts about them [here](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JiWTjLo2zxsqf2YXF/interpretability-is-becoming-increasingly-uninterpretable) (fortuitously, [this post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LQXWiF8PyJ5ojNsEv/how-robust-are-natural-language-autoencoders-to) was released a few days later). 

To be clear, I do think investment in interpretability is a good idea; my opinions here are largely influenced by personal taste (though also somewhat by a desire for pragmatism and trustworthiness). It just seems that my interest is somewhere in the middle of the abstraction spectrum.
