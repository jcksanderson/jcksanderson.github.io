Dr. Steiner received little screen time in his first two appearances on *The Mentalist.* In both cases, Patrick Jane---the show's protagonist---humored himself by messing with him. Dr. Steiner's third appearance was different. The episode started the same as the previous two featuring Dr. Steiner, with Jane messing with him. But then the body was stolen. 

This is probably a good time to mention that Dr. Steiner is a coroner, and Jane a consultant for the California Bureau of Investigation (CBI). They were both investigating a murder. While transporting a body back to his office, he was held up at gunpoint by two people who stole the body and fled. 

For Dr. Steiner, this was devastating.

> The body is my responsibility. My reason for being here. Now I have no reason or responsibility. There's nothing for me to do. I don't expect you to understand why that feels like failure to me, but it does.

***

Dr. Steiner revisits the CBI's office to bring news that they had picked up two distinct DNA signatures at the crime scene. Jane asks Dr. Steiner why he came to the office to deliver the news, as opposed to making a phone call. 

> I would like to assist in some small way, if that's alright.

Jane obliges. Dr. Steiner is clearly nervous about being there---this is not his element---but within a few minutes contributes a useful idea. For the most part, though, he simply follows along.

***

While at the victim's house, Jane and Dr. Steiner slip away to the study, where Jane pours the two some scotch. 

> You know that I'm sick. Why would Patrick Jane, of all people... invite me to a fancy house... and put a first-rate Scotch in my hand if he didn't know? [...] I'm dying. I have a month, most likely. Maybe less.

Subtly, but visibly, Jane saddens. He had correctly reasoned that Dr. Steiner was sick, but was not expected such a short timeline.

***

Eventually, Jane gets Dr. Steiner to claim that he's recovered the body and has all the evidence he needs to close the case, but is willing to sell the body to the killer for $50,000. After all, he's dying.

Dr. Steiner anxiously waits at the agreed pickup location. The killer shows up, and is arrested by the onlooking CBI agents.

***

With the investigation over, Dr. Steiner invites Jane to his home. He thanks Jane, who responds that it was his pleasure. Dr. Steiner then tells Jayne that he is going to kill himself.

> Um, look... won't beat around the bush. About me dying. I know what's going to happen now. I've seen it. There are no mysteries. It's going to get bad very soon... and then worse. And I have no interest in letting nature take its course. You understand?

But Dr. Steiner, after so many years of being a coroner, does not want to be autopsied himself. To avoid the autopsy, he needs a law-enforcement officer to witness his death.

> I have pills I can take. The problem is, if there is an unexplained death... if a body is found in an apartment... there is an investigation. An autopsy. And the idea of being examined... of being the body on the table.... But if the death is witnessed by a law-enforcement officer... no investigation is required.

Jane is quite uncomfortable with this, says so, and starts to leave. But then he asks if he can make a cup of tea. 

Dr. Steiner shows him to the kitchen, points out the tea and cookies, then leaves to another room for a bit.

> Well, uh... I'm going in the other room now. I'll be a few minutes.

After those few minutes, Jane joins Dr. Steiner, who is sitting tensely on the side of the couch with the face of a man projecting courage and conviction, as if his family were looking to him for hope in an otherwise hopeless situation.

Dr. Steiner asks Jane if he had the deputy plant evidence on the body to pull the stunt at the beginning of the episode. Jane confirms: "It's an old carny magic trick."

Dr. Steiner then asks Jane about his background in magic. Jane shows him a simple coin trick. "See that coin? It's there, right? And it's gone."

Dr. Steiner's grip starts to loosen, and Jane takes his teacup and places it on the table.

"It's there. And it's gone."

"Patrick. Thank you," Dr. Steiner barely gets out.

Jane continues: "Just watch the coin. And it's gone. It's there. And then it's gone."

Dr. Steiner then passed away, peacefully.

***

I'm posting this 8 weeks after I initially wrote it. I think there's something very profound in Dr. Steiner's need to have purpose in the days leading up to his death. This need for purpose is not a novel idea by any stretch of the imagination, but the episode did a great job at conveying it, especially because of its pairing with Jane's changing attitude toward Dr. Steiner. I found it quite moving, and maybe you would too.
